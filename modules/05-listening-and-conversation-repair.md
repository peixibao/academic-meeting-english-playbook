# 05 · Listening, clarification and repair

**Missed the words:** “Sorry, I didn't quite catch the last part. Could you say that again?”

**Too fast:** “Would you mind speaking a little more slowly?”

**Know the words but not the question:** “Could you rephrase the question?”

**Unfamiliar technical term:** “I'm not familiar with that term in this context. Could you explain how you're using it?”

**Check understanding:** “Just to make sure I've got this right, you're suggesting [REPHRASE]. Is that correct?”

**Thinking time:** “That's a good question. Let me think for a moment.”

**No direct experience:** “I haven't used that method myself yet. I'd be interested to learn how it works in this setting.”

**Connection:** “Sorry, the audio cut out for a second. I missed what you said after [WORD].”

## Three-stage strategy

1. Ask to repeat.
2. If still unclear, specify the exact unclear part.
3. Restate your understanding before answering.

Do not nod along to a methodological claim you did not understand. Clarification is a normal part of academic dialogue.

### Practice
Simulate one unfamiliar acronym, one interrupted sentence, and one broad question that needs a narrower definition.
