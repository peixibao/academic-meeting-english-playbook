# Lesson 05 · Unexpected Situations & Recovery

## Where this lesson fits

This is Unit 5 of the **fixed eight-unit Academic Meeting English Playbook**. Its purpose is to help you keep a professional conversation productive when something unexpected happens: words are missed, meaning is unclear, you need time to think, a misunderstanding occurs, or technology fails.

Earlier lessons introduced a few useful expressions. Lesson 05 provides their full grammar, pragmatic nuances, and practical recovery strategies.

## Rapid-reference decision tree

1. **Couldn't hear the words?** → “Sorry, I didn't quite catch that. Could you repeat the last part?” (5.1)
2. **Heard the words but didn't follow the reasoning?** → “I heard the part about the first model, but I didn't quite follow the next step.” (5.2)
3. **Understood the question but need time?** → “That's a good question. Let me think for a moment.” (5.3)
4. **Don't know or need to correct something?** → “I don't know the exact figure offhand.” / “Sorry, let me correct that.” (5.3)
5. **Audio, video or connection failed?** → “The audio cut out for a second. I missed what came after ‘recruitment.’” (5.4)

## Three-stage recovery strategy

From the existing GitHub listening-repair reference:

1. Ask for repetition when the words are missed.
2. If still unclear, specify exactly which part remains unclear.
3. Restate your understanding and invite correction before answering.

If sound repetition will not solve the problem, request a rephrase, explanation, example, or step-by-step walkthrough.
