# Lesson 07 · Expressing Expectations & Negotiating

**Unit 7 of the fixed eight-unit Academic Meeting English Playbook**

## Where Lesson 07 fits

Lessons 01–05 cover meeting interaction, pragmatic politeness, turn management and recovery. Lesson 06 focuses on research and program discussion, including motivation and academic fit. **Lesson 07 develops the mutual-expectations and negotiation stage**: what each side expects, what support and resources exist, what an opportunity actually involves, and how to make an informed decision without accidental commitments.

A preliminary meeting is not necessarily an admission, employment or scholarship negotiation. Adapt questions to the opportunity's actual stage. All sample dialogues are fictional; degree-specific policies require current official confirmation.

## Decision map: Which section should you open?

- **“What are you hoping to gain from this degree?”** → **7.1**; develop specific training aims and describe needed feedback.
- **“What would the student actually be responsible for?”** → **7.2**; role, reporting lines and deliverables.
- **“Will the PI take me? Who pays for the degree?”** → **7.3**; capacity, admissions authority and funding rules are different questions.
- **“I don't think that plan works for me.”** → **7.4**; substantive but respectful disagreement, and a clear boundary.
- **“Do I actually want to join this group?”** → **7.5**; independent investigation and two-way evaluation.

## Five distinctions to remember

1. **Interest ≠ commitment.** “I'd be interested in exploring it” does not mean “I accept.”
2. **Student training ≠ unlimited tasks.** Clarify scope, expected milestones and support.
3. **Publication potential ≠ guaranteed paper or authorship order.** Discuss expected contributions and relevant standards.
4. **Supervisor encouragement ≠ formal admission or funding award.** Identify decision authorities and written conditions.
5. **Research-topic fit ≠ supervision or workplace fit.** Evaluate these separately.
