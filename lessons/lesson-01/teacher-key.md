# Lesson 01 · Instructor key and instructional rules

## What the Columbia PDF actually says

- First prospective advisor meetings are **mutual scientific-interest and mentor-fit conversations** after an introductory email.
- Part A divides *answer preparation* into: mutual interests; skills/qualities; goals; financial support; handling inappropriate questions (US law references should not be generalized).
- Part A divides *question preparation* into: project development/management; financial support; departmental/lab culture; availability; communication style.
- It encourages discussion with faculty and students, clear expectations, and not rushing decisions after a meeting.
- Some first-meeting questions may be deferred if time runs short.
- The PDF was prepared by Columbia E3B graduate students, mainly for **US ecology/evolution PhD**. It isn't a universal official policy or a Canadian HRM MSc interview script.

Original PDF: https://e3b.columbia.edu/sites/e3b.columbia.edu/files/content/Guide-on-Meeting-with-Potential-Advisor-and-Asking-Questions-of-a-Grad-Program.pdf

## Source-anchored study: acceptable short answer ideas

1. Faculty use the meeting to learn student's interest and background, assess research fit; applicants assess guidance/communication fit.
2. Learners can judge whether their scientific interests align with group projects and whether the PI listens and asks meaningful follow-ups.
3. A fixed thesis question is not mandatory in Columbia's US PhD context; developing desired research skills is an acceptable goal. Check actual HRM MSc expectations separately.

## Pedagogy corrections (important)

The learner explicitly asked for **handbook-style practical training** (what to expect, what the professor asks, what to ask, example responses) and rejected A1 English videos and specialist podcast comprehension as the opening course unit. Don't switch focus again. Use university guide as INPUT, teaching explanations/examples second, guided Q&A third, role-play last. Supplement methodology vocabulary only where essential to an answer.

## Revision and speaking feedback

Capture exact learner original speech; explain unnatural wording and errors; give both academically safe and natural North American spoken alternatives. Preserve accurate research contributions, do not invent projects/funding.

## Completion

Only change lesson status after the learner has read, studied models, attempted guided questions and completed a live two-way mini meeting.
