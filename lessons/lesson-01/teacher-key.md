Keep the material **English-first, rigorous, plain-Markdown, and transferable**. Explain pragmatic choices and related conversational branches together. Pronunciation exchanges must include both participants' uncertainty, confirming a correct reading, and tactfully correcting an incorrect one.

The Columbia graduate students' guide on meeting prospective advisors is a useful **later topic-specific supplement**, not the mandatory basis for greetings or basic small talk: https://e3b.columbia.edu/sites/e3b.columbia.edu/files/content/Guide-on-Meeting-with-Potential-Advisor-and-Asking-Questions-of-a-Grad-Program.pdf

Do not use beginner quizzes as a substitute for teaching. Offer free production practice when appropriate. In feedback: preserve the learner's intended meaning, explain wording and register, and provide both polished professional and natural spoken versions when useful.

Do not claim that reviewing notes alone demonstrates full speaking proficiency. Do not invent a learner's experience, a professor's words, project availability, or university rules.
