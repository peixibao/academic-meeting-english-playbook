# Lesson 08 · Wrapping Up & Follow-up

**Unit 8 of the fixed eight-unit Academic Meeting English Playbook**

## Where this unit fits

Lesson 08 completes the meeting conversation: ending respectfully, distinguishing what was discussed from what was actually agreed, documenting actions, following up by email, and handling an encouraging, unclear or negative outcome without inventing an offer.

This follows Lesson 07's work on supervision expectations, roles, funding and boundaries. It remains suitable for prospective-supervisor and research-group conversations, with attention to differences between informal meetings, admissions interviews and funding processes. Institutional eligibility, application deadlines, funding decisions and supervisor authority must be verified separately.

## Rapid-reference: What do I need to do?

1. **“Is there anything else you'd like to ask?”** → 8.1: acknowledge, ask one important final question or close.
2. **“Please send me your CV.”** → 8.1: confirm scope; 8.2: send the requested document.
3. **“Thanks for talking. Let's stay in touch.”** → 8.1 and 8.3: avoid assuming a commitment; ask for an appropriate follow-up step only if useful.
4. **“I sent my email but haven't heard back.”** → 8.2: contextual, well-timed reminder; 8.3: decide when to continue elsewhere.
5. **“Your background is relevant, but funding is uncertain.”** → 8.3: distinguish genuine scientific interest from a funded opening.
6. **“I'm not taking students this year.”** → 8.3: acknowledge professionally and consider one targeted referral question.
7. **“The formal application is reviewed by a committee.”** → 8.3: follow the actual authorized process, not assumptions based on a meeting.

## Key boundaries

- An enthusiastic discussion is not a formal admission or funding offer.
- A suggestion is not necessarily a confirmed task.
- A polite “let's stay in touch” is not a scheduled appointment.
- A draft or unpublished research item is not appropriate to share without authorization.
- A general email reminder cannot replace checking official program deadlines or procedures.
- A saved lesson is not evidence of fluency; real practice can follow independently.
