# Lesson 06 · Topic-Specific Discussion (Replaceable)

## Purpose and fixed-syllabus placement

This is Unit 6 of the **unchanged eight-unit Academic Meeting English Playbook**. Earlier lessons teach broadly transferable meeting language. This is the **prospective-supervisor / research-discussion version** of the replaceable topic-specific module. It is reusable for speaking about different scientific fields and does not lock the learner into a particular research specialty.

The module trains **two-way scientific discussion**, not memorized interviews, admissions guarantees, or a fixed speech about a particular professor. Sample projects, populations, study designs and numerical placeholders are illustrative only; replace them with real, checked information.

## Quick reference: which section do I need?

1. **“Tell me about yourself.”** → 6.1: brief academic orientation, verified role, current interest.
2. **“Walk me through your study.”** → 6.2: question → design → analysis → finding → limitation.
3. **“Did you read any of my papers?”** → 6.3: identify the actual paper, one meaningful detail and one substantive question.
4. **“Do you have a research idea?”** → 6.4: one tentative question, potential data/design, uncertainty about feasibility.
5. **“Would you want to work on this?”** → 6.4: express genuine interest and request detail; reserve a formal decision until you understand the opportunity. Continue to Lesson 07 for negotiation.
6. **“Why an MPhil, why a PhD, and why this country/university/program/supervisor?”** → 6.5: explain genuine reasons for your choices and connect them with verified program information.

## Scope boundary: Lesson 06 vs. Lesson 07

**Lesson 06:** Scientific questions, research background, papers, analytical reasoning, possible projects, tentative fit, and explaining the motivation for degree/country/university/program/supervisor selection.

**Lesson 07:** Mutual expectations, negotiating responsibilities and boundaries, handling disagreement, project and supervision terms, follow-up conditions and commitments.

Overlap in everyday language is natural; the teaching goals remain distinct.
