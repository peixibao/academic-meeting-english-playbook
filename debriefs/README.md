# Academic Meeting Debriefs

A **separate, reusable reflection track** for turning real academic conversations into transferable knowledge and better next-meeting preparation.

## What to record after a meeting

| Layer | Diagnostic question | Next action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Conceptual knowledge** | Did I understand the discipline-specific term itself? | Learn the concept in plain language; provide an example and a counterexample. |
| **Listening** | Did I recognize the words and reconstruct the meaning in real time? | Practice paraphrasing a 20–40 second explanation. |
| **Intent recognition** | What decision or information was the other person actually asking for? | State the question's purpose before answering. |
| **Spoken response** | Was the answer concise, accurate and relevant? | Write a 20-second answer and an extended 60-second version. |
| **Research alignment** | Do our research questions, methods and daily work match? | Ask specific fit questions instead of relying on broad labels. |
| **Repair strategy** | Did I ask for clarification before the confusion grew? | Prepare a direct, polite clarification sentence. |

**Principle:** A fluent-sounding answer can still miss the question. A clear clarification request is often more useful than guessing.

## Privacy and publication rules

**This is a public learning resource.** Entries must be synthesized and non-identifying.

- Do not include names of professors, collaborators or institutions.
- Do not include meeting dates/times, original transcripts, recordings, email addresses, private correspondence, meeting links, or identifiable direct quotations.
- Do not describe a particular research group's actual projects, data, funding, unpublished plans, or identifiable specialty.
- Use broad educational concepts and **fictional or reconstructed** example exchanges, clearly labeled as such.
- Keep sensitive or highly personal meeting notes in a private document, outside this repository.
- Review new cases for re-identification risk before publishing.

## Debrief naming

`001-short-learning-theme.md`, `002-short-learning-theme.md`, etc. Number by **learning case**, not by professor or meeting date.

## Next-meeting rule

Before every discussion: **define the terms → understand the professor's published work privately → state your interests → prepare fit questions → practice repair phrases.**
