# 00 · How a prospective-supervisor meeting works

## Before

- Confirm day, exact time and the professor's time zone. The organizer will usually provide a video link or calendar invite; don't assume the app.
- Check microphone, camera, internet, display name and any lobby access a day before.
- Keep a CV, two short research examples, one relevant paper, three questions and a blank notes page nearby.
- If no agenda was supplied: “Please let me know if there is anything in particular you'd like me to prepare ahead of our meeting.”

## Possible 20–30-minute flow (illustration, never a promised agenda)

| Phase | Your task | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Join and greet | Test sound; polite small talk | “Can you hear me okay?” |
| Introductions | Explain degree and research focus briefly | “I have mainly worked with observational studies.” |
| Research exchange | Discuss paper, question, method and evidence limits | “What interested me was how the design affected interpretation.” |
| Explore projects | Ask and listen; offer tentative ideas | “Would there be room for an MSc student to develop a question?” |
| Mutual expectations | Ask focused questions rather than a checklist | “How do you normally work with MSc students?” |
| Close | Confirm the actionable next step | “Just to confirm, I should send you ...?” |

An informal first meeting can still inform whether each side sees a fit. A professor might begin with their project or with a question about you. Be prepared to follow either path.

## During

Answer the question, pause, and ask a meaningful follow-up. If a term is unclear, ask for clarification immediately. Keep brief, private notes instead of transcribing every sentence.

## After

Write your own record of: papers/projects mentioned; research responsibilities; unresolved questions; next steps; dates. Send a short follow-up if appropriate. Do not interpret a polite invitation as an offer of admission.
