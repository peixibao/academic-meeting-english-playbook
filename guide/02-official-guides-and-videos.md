# 02 · Source-checked guide and media library

These are external official resources; we link and summarize them, not reproduce full text.

## Graduate-supervisor guidance

1. **Waterloo School of Public Health Sciences — Topics to Discuss with Your Potential Supervisor**  
https://uwaterloo.ca/public-health-sciences/future-graduate-students/topics-discuss-your-potential-supervisor  
Practical topics: team composition, availability, communication expectations and funding. Suggested for use beyond the first meeting.

2. **McGill Graduate Studies — Connecting with a Supervisor**  
https://www.mcgill.ca/gradapplicants/research-supervision/connecting-supervisor  
Two-way evaluation: research interests, student outcomes, supervisory style, publishing and expectations.

3. **Harvard Undergraduate Research — Preparing for the First Meeting**  
https://uraf.harvard.edu/resource/preparing-first-meeting  
Before/during/after: read a recent paper, bring questions, listen, take notes, follow up. A mentor-meeting guide, not a specific MSc admission policy.

4. **UBC Graduate School — Supervision**  
https://www.grad.ubc.ca/supervision  
Discuss supervision, feedback, funding, meetings and authorship; particularly relevant once a possible collaboration becomes concrete.

5. **McMaster Graduate Studies — Supervision Guidelines**  
https://gs.mcmaster.ca/current-students/resources/graduate-supervision/supervision-guidelines-for-supervisors/  
Institutional framework for graduate research supervision. Doesn't dictate the agenda of a prospective student's informal meeting.

## Medical publication norms

**ICMJE — Defining the Role of Authors and Contributors:**  
https://icmje.org/recommendations/browse/roles-and-responsibilities/defining-the-role-of-authors-and-contributors.html  
For medical journals, the four authorship criteria involve substantial scholarly contribution, drafting/critical review, final approval, and accountability. Authorship order is a collective decision. Discuss contributions and expectations early without demanding guaranteed first authorship.

## How to use a video

Watch → notice three functional phrases → repeat them aloud → replace nouns and contexts with a realistic academic situation → apply in an unscripted role-play. Log the phrase, never copy a whole transcript into this repository.
